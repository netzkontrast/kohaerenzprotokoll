## Scene protocol

1. Read the target chapter file in full, including Summary/Outline.
2. Read previous and next chapter; for continuity-heavy scenes also read the later payoff chapter.
3. Identify one primary scene concept. Do not stack theory, reveal, trauma cue, world mechanics and character pivot in one scene.
4. Define: entry state → concrete action → contradiction/pressure → exit state.
5. Apply the POV's Sprach-DNA and somatic signature; never label a part/voice transition in prose.
6. Enforce sensory polarity: cold/sharp ozone = AEGIS suppression; warmth = Juna/relational trace; do not mix them in one scene unless a later locked Vortex beat explicitly requires convergence.
7. Keep theory submerged. The reader experiences mechanisms through work, objects, timing, space, body, logs and omission.
8. After drafting, compare factual details against adjacent chapters: times, counts, object state, wording of directives, data fields, who knows what, and what has already been erased/preserved.

## Hard prohibitions

- No explanatory diagnosis in early Arc I.
- No speaker headers for inner parts.
- No direct emotional self-report where Kael's register should externalize it through body, counting or procedure.
- No new lore solely to make a scene feel richer.
- No continuity change without checking its downstream log/data consequences.
