# Q3 — how many, and do they correspond?

Seven pages ask some form of this, and it has been open since document 1.

## What is known

| | |
|---|---|
| Kern-Welten named | **four** — [[konstrukt-stadt|Konstrukt-Stadt]], [[resonanz-landschaft|Resonanz-Landschaft]], [[grenzfeste|Grenzfeste]], [[moeglichkeits-garten|Möglichkeits-Garten]] ^[guardians-und-kern-welten-konzept.md:L35] |
| whether four is all of them | **not stated by any read source** |
| [[alters]] | the [[alters|Alters]] page records the corpus's largest disagreement |
| the correspondence | raised by document 1, unanswered by three documents since |

The premise that makes it matter is stated: [[kael|Kael]]'s fragmented psyche is
represented as simulated worlds ^[entropie-aegis.md:L21]. If one world is one
alter, the two counts are the same count.

## Why it is one question

The Kern-Welt count, the alter count and the correspondence cannot be settled
separately — settling any two settles the third, and a document that answers one
almost certainly touches the others.

## What would answer it

A source that states a count, or that states the correspondence as a rule. Four
named worlds is not four worlds; the document that named them was describing
Guardian pairs, and a fifth world with no Guardian would not have appeared in it.

## 2026-09-17 — document 6 answers the first half and leaves the other two open

`roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung` is read. **It is the first source
that bounds the Kern-Welt count from outside**, by counting the level above them.

### The count: four Kern-Welten, inside six Realitätsebenen

> „Eindeutige Bezeichnung und Zuordnung zu einer der sechs Realitätsebenen
> (KW1-4, Überwelt, Externe Ebene)."
> ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L105]

The phrase „sechs Realitätsebenen" occurs five times across five sections
^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L17], ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L166],
^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L250], ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L549],
never qualified, and every one of the document's 51 locations is assigned to
exactly one of the six. The four are numbered KW1–KW4, each with a domain bearer
and a psychological function ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L172–L175].

**So „four named worlds is not four worlds" now has an answer from one source:
four is the count, because the frame that holds them has six members and two of
them are not [[kern-welten|Kern-Welten]].** One source, cataloguing rather than defining, and
nothing contradicts it. See [[realitaetsebenen]].

### The alter count is not answered, and the wording is why

Each level names an Alter with „wie":

> „Assoziiert mit Alters wie Limina." ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L172]

and three more like it ^[roman-lokalitaeten-konzept-und-ausarbeitung.md:L173–L175]. **„wie" is
„such as".** Four [[alters|Alters]] are attested — `Limina`, `Echo`, `Nox`, `Orakel/Muse` —
and no level's roster is closed, so the document is compatible with 11, with 13
and with any other number.

### The correspondence is suggested by arrangement and stated nowhere

One Alter per level, four levels, four [[alters|Alters]] — the shape of a one-to-one rule,
written as four examples. The document never says a Kern-Welt corresponds to an
Alter, and it also names NPCs per level in the same clauses, which a strict
correspondence would not.

**Q3 therefore splits.** The world count has an answer to check against the next
source; the alter count and the correspondence are where they were.

## 2026-09-17 — a fourth world-count confirmation, and a third alter roster that breaks the shape

`kohaerenz-protokoll-konzept` (2025-04-17) is read. It never writes `Kern-Welt`
— see [[kern-welten]] for its own four world-names — but independently confirms
four simulated worlds inside six total levels
^[kohaerenz-protokoll-konzept.md:L39, L168].

### The alter roster no longer looks like a one-to-one rule at all

This document names seven, explicitly hedged as sketch/example
^[kohaerenz-protokoll-konzept.md:L110], and puts **two** in one world and **two**
in another rather than one per world:

| world | [[alters]] named here |
|---|---|
| Welt 1 | „Schatten" (Persecutor), unnamed Memory Holder (EP) |
| Welt 2 | „Leo" (Kind-Anteil) |
| Welt 3 | „Wächter" (Protector) |
| Welt 4 | Michael (Host), optional „Funke" (ISH) |

Document 6's roster was four names, one per world, with `wie` leaving every
level open — compatible with a one-to-one rule even if it did not state one.
**This document's roster is not compatible with a one-to-one rule at all**,
because two named alters share a world twice. Three documents, three rosters,
zero shared names (`Limina`/`Echo`/`Nox`/`Orakel-Muse` vs
`Host`/`Wächter`/`Schatten`/`Leo`/`Der Archivar`/`Memory Holder`/`Funke`, and
neither overlaps the disputed 11-or-13 count's sources).

**What this adds:** the "one alter per world" shape was always an
unstated pattern in the data available, never a stated rule. A document that
states a rule, or a fourth roster that also shares no name with the first
three, would now be needed to settle whether any correspondence holds at all —
the possibility space has widened, not narrowed. See [[alters]].
