# Kishōtenketsu

**A borrowed structure — the Japanese four-part narrative Ki, Shō, Ten, Ketsu, built
without a central conflict — which the 2026 sources make the novel's overarching
logic of meaning: the turn gives the earlier chapters their meaning afterwards, and
the ending is a synthesis rather than a victory.** Where each part falls in the
chapters is laid out per source in the plot overview
([plot.md](../overview/plot.md)); this page holds what the sources say the structure
is and does.

## Where the sources differ

**What the ending leaves open.** The Three-Mode Architecture (2026-05-08, 39 chapters)
makes the reset one of the two outcomes the Kishōtenketsu ending itself holds open:
a triumph or „eine Sisyphos-Erkenntnis der Heilung-durch-Reset" ^[three-mode-architecture-39-chapters-md.md:L315], and the reader decides.
The Kapitel-Kompendium, the storyform outline and the glossary, under the lock of
2026-05-30, give the reset to the conflict reader and the transfiguration to the
Kishōtenketsu reader: „Reset bleibt Leser-Projektion, nie In-Welt-Tatsache." ^[kapitel-kompendium-gather-2026-05-31-md.md:L56]
There the reset is not a Kishōtenketsu reading at all. They agree that the text
never decides. The earlier plan's ending is Kap 39, and the later ones' is Kap 40.

**Where conflict enters.** The Kap-0 annotation of 2026-05-17 has Ki as Kap 0 alone
in its sense of establishment and says „Konflikt entsteht in Akt I (Kap 1 ff.)." ^[kap0-v1-annotiert-md.md:L1177]
The strukturierter Outline, a day later, counts all of Akt I in Ki, which
„Setzt die Welt und das Substrat ohne externen Konflikt-Motor." ^[koharenz-protokoll-strukturierter-outline-2026-05-18-md.md:L29]
The glossary says the whole structure „ohne zentralen Konflikt operiert" ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-begriffe-und-konzepte-2026-06-10-md.md:L566].

**Replacing the acts, or above them.** The Hard-Problem analysis has Kishōtenketsu
replace the three-act model (its L29). The strukturierter Outline keeps three modes
and three acts and puts Kishōtenketsu above them as a „Top-Level-Klammer" ^[koharenz-protokoll-strukturierter-outline-2026-05-18-md.md:L23],
and the glossary counts it as one of four stage systems, the one that gives their
sense (its L575).

**Where the parts fall.** Ki, Shō, Ten and Ketsu are mapped onto different chapters
by the strukturierter Outline and by the Kapitel-Kompendium, the storyform outline
and the glossary. The plot overview has the mapping per source.
