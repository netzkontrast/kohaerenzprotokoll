# Juna

**In document 1 she is called Julia**, and unlike `Michael`, that old name goes
on being used deliberately for months.

## Reading — `entropie-aegis`, 2025-04-17, brief

She is defined here **only as a position**, not as a person:

> „die Verbindung zu einer externen, nicht-digitalen Ebene (im Roman
> repräsentiert durch \"**Julia**\")" ^[entropie-aegis.md:L71]

That is the whole content: she *represents* a connection to a non-digital layer
outside the simulation. The quotation marks around the name are the document's
own, marking that the name stands for something rather than naming someone.

Her second appearance is as the other side of the central conflict, paired with
Michael ^[entropie-aegis.md:L96].

## The rename, and why hers is different

The cut is the same single day as [[kael|Kael]] — 2025-04-17 all Julia, 2025-04-18 all
Juna, no mixed document either side.

But afterwards the two names behave differently:

| | documents after the cut | shape |
|---|--:|---|
| `Michael` | 16 | 9 of them use it **exactly once** — leftovers |
| `Julia` | 20 | median **2**, maximum **34** |

One document written two days after the cut uses **[[kael|Kael]] 45 times and Julia 34
times.** That is not a missed find-and-replace. Something kept the old name for
her alive.

**This page does not say what.** Three readings are available and none is
supported: the research briefs were written against older context; „Julia" names
the external person while „Juna" names her in-simulation representation; or the
rename simply took longer to stick for her. The evidence distinguishes none of
them.
