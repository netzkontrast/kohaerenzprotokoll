## Open while reading

The document is a plan for a writer, not finished text: it speaks in the imperative and
the conditional (`müssen wir`, `sollte`, `könnte`, `Nutze`, `Integriere`), and it takes the
twist of its first sentence as a given without saying whether that twist is decided. It
carries no date, no status label and no claim about its own authority in the body. Two
titles stand on it: the manifest's `Meta-Foreshadowing & Beobachter-Logik` and the H1's
`Foreshadowing-Strategie: Die Beobachter-Instanz`. Neither `Meta-Foreshadowing` nor
`Beobachter-Logik` is written in the body, so neither is listed.

The document labels its passages in bold — a fixed pair `Das Foreshadowing` and `Die
Meta-Erklärung` in sections 1 and 2, `Das Foreshadowing` and `Limit-Integration` in
section 3, `Der Dialog-Hinweis` and `Die Umkehrung` in section 4, `Annotation` and `Der
Schock` in section 5, `Perspektivwechsel` and `Typografie` under the H3, and one
`Text-Beispiel` in section 1. Those labels say how to read the passage after them, so they
belong to the note's stance markers. Only the two that repeat stand on the list —
`Das Foreshadowing` carries the H1's own word and `Die Meta-Erklärung` is its counterpart —
and the other eight are left off it on purpose.

Four voices are given as samples in italics and quotation marks: a narrating voice the
text does not name (L21), Juna (L41, named by the sentence that introduces it), Kael's inner
voice (L53, named by the sentence that introduces it) and a last italic line after the
`end list` marker (L58) that names no speaker. The word for the reader is missing from the narrator's sample
and from Juna's, which render the reader as `Jemand` and `da draußen jemand`; both are
listed because the text writes them, and whether they mean the reader is the reading's to
say. The last line also sits after an export artefact, so what it belongs to is not marked.

Used as known and defined nowhere here: `Teil 1`, `Teil 2`, `Teil 3` (the sections are
numbered 1 to 5 and the parts 1 to 3, two series), `Konstrukt`, `Signal`, `Höhere Metrik`
(a heading whose phrase the body never repeats), `Trauma`, and the cited document titled
`Grenzen der Mathematik`, which is the title of a cited work and stays off the list. The
words `Welt`, `Zeit`, `Buch`, `Herz`, `Zeilen`, `Wahrheit` and `Schicksal` are ordinary
nouns here and stay off too, though the world's names are several — `Welt`, `Konstrukt`,
`System`, `Universums` — and the document never says they are one thing.

Borderline calls, made by the rule and not by memory of anything else: `Sonde`, `Werkzeug`
and `Manifestation des Lesers` are ordinary nouns used as what Kael is, so they are listed
as role names; `System` names what AEGIS administers and may shut off; `autonomer Gott` is
restated only to be rejected; `Trauma` and `Dissoziation` are borrowed psychology applied
to the reader's relation to the world, so they stand under lens with `unentscheidbar` and
the heat death.
