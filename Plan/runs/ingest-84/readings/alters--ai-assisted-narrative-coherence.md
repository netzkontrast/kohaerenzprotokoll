## Reading — `ai-assisted-narrative-coherence`, 2025-10-15, the blueprint, the Kael biography and the architecture analysis of the English compilation — three alter tables of different lengths, and a stated count of eleven

The compilation names the parts of Kael's psyche as `Alters` ^[ai-assisted-narrative-coherence.md:#12] (capitalised) and `alters` ^[ai-assisted-narrative-coherence.md:#43] (lowercase). Its introductory texts define them: the ARCHON proposal says the protagonist's „Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) is modeled on the“ ^[?] Theory of Structural Dissociation, and the simple guide (L219 on) says „His consciousness is split into multiple distinct parts, or identities,“ ^[?] and goes on to call them Alters.

The counts differ by part:

- The blueprint (L339 on) states the number in prose: „the existence of eleven identified alters“ ^[?]. Its table follows under „The following table profiles the most significant alters“ ^[?] and has six rows, Kael, Lex, Nyx, Kiko, Rhys and Selene (L440–L445).
- The Kael biography (L1073 on) says „The table below profiles the five most significant alters“ ^[?]; its table lists Kael, Lex, Nyx, Kiko and Selene (L1106–L1110).
- The architecture analysis (L1671 on) opens its table with „composed of distinct personality parts, or alters.“ ^[?] and has eleven rows, Kael, Selene, Nyx, Kiko, Lia, Isabelle, Moros, Lex, Alex, Rhys and Argus (L1754–L1764).

Lia, Isabelle, Moros, Alex and Argus are named in the scene outline (L1273 on) at L1312, L1383, L1516, L1542 and L1555, and appear in the last table.
