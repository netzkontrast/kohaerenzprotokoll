## Reading — `an-architecture-of-the-self-a-critical-review-of-kohaerenz-p`, 2025-10-15, the critical review — what the critical review reports of the protagonist and judges of the premise

The critical review reports that the novel builds on a `unified protagonist` (the review's own term) and describes it as „the seamless externalization of the protagonist Kael“ ^[?], with the protagonist's condition named in `TSDP`; the glitches of the simulated reality are, it says, „a direct, causal manifestation of it“ ^[?]. It reports that „The novel posits that the world itself is a function of the protagonist“ ^[?], and that this fusion is what lifts the work from allegory to a narrative engine.

On the two orders, the review reports that „Kael discovers a form of order that emerges from the acceptance and integration of contradictory parts“ ^[?], set against AEGIS's order. It reports the final act: „ultimate role is not as a destroyer but as“ ^[?] a gardener figure, and judges the resolution doubtful (it asks whether the narrative earns it). On the system of parts it reports „The ultimate goal is not the erasure of parts in a forced“ ^[?] fusion but functional multiplicity.

- The critical review reports the goal of the protagonist's development as functional multiplicity rather than fusion, and judges that the novel largely succeeds; this is its verdict, not a canon claim.
