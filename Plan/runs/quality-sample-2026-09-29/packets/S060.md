### The claim as written on the page

**Four Kernwelten, a Guardian and an IFS category each, and eleven Alters distributed to keep narrative noise down — a stated rule, not a suggestion.**
A report addressed to the author; recorded, not applied. „Um narratives Rauschen zu vermeiden, werden die elf identifizierten Alters rigoros den vier Kernwelten und deren Guardians zugeordnet" ^[roman-konzept-dualitaet-kohaerenz-spannung.md:L33] — the correspondence this question asks after, stated as the document's own organising principle rather than left implicit. The table (L40–L43): „Konstrukt-Stadt (KW1) | LogOS (Rationalismus) | Manager (ANP) | Kael (Host), Lex (Analytiker), Limina (Gatekeeper)" ^[roman-konzept-dualitaet-kohaerenz-spannung.md:L40], „Resonanz-Landschaft (KW2) | Mnemosyne (Erinnerung) | Manager & Caretaker | Silas (Pfleger), Eos (Soziale Manipulation)" ^[roman-konzept-dualitaet-kohaerenz-spannung.md:L41], „Grenzfeste (KW3) | Cerberus (Abwehr/Angst) | Firefighter (Persecutor) & Exiles | Nyx (Kampf), Praetor (Abwehr), Nox (Kritiker)" ^[roman-konzept-dualitaet-kohaerenz-spannung.md:L42], „Möglichkeits-Garten (KW4) | Kairos & Sophia | Exiles (Trauma-Halter) | Kiko (Kind/Flucht), Oblivion (Kollaps), Juna (Anomalie)" ^[roman-konzept-dualitaet-kohaerenz-spannung.md:L43].
So this document answers what document 6 left as „wie" (examples, not a closed roster) with a rule stated in the opposite direction: not four worlds each holding some alters, but eleven alters each assigned to exactly one world — a one-to-one correspondence at the group level, four to three alters per world, not one each. `Limina`, `Eos`, `Praetor` and `Nox` have no page of their own; see [[alters]].
