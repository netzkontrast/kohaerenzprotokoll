## What this says

**Five models held the schema perfectly, and two of them are free.**
`nex-n2.5-pro:free` and `dots-3-note-preview:free` returned schema-valid,
fully-scoring predictions on every attempt. For the `worker` role — bulk
sub-steps and LM-as-judge — that is the result worth acting on.

**`ling-3.0-flash` is the standout on the paid side:** perfect score at 24.3 s,
the fastest of the five perfect scorers and roughly a third of `gpt-oss-120b`'s
latency for the same quality.

**The two low scorers failed on quality, not structure.** `granite-4.0-h-micro`
(0.75) and `mistral-nemo` (0.73) emitted valid schemas every time and simply
extracted worse claims. That is the more useful kind of failure: it degrades
visibly rather than crashing, and `ingest_metric` catches it.

**The one partial failure is instructive.** `nemotron-3.5-lightning:free` scored
1.00 when it worked but failed one attempt in three with
`AdapterParseError: LM response cannot be serialized to a JSON object`, and the
response began *"Here's a thinking process:"* — a reasoning model emitting its
chain of thought where a JSON object belongs. This is exactly the failure mode
the benchmark was built to catch, and exactly why `--repeats 1` would have been
worthless: a single lucky attempt would have shown 100 %.

Its 245 s median also disqualifies it independently. That is 10× `ling-3.0-flash`
for the same score.

## Caveats — read before acting

1. **One program, one small fixture.** `SourceIngest` on a 4-line German note.
   `BatchCompile` has six predictors and a substantially larger merge step; a
   model that holds this schema may still fail there. A good result here means
   *worth a real trial run*, not *safe to adopt*.
2. **n = 3.** A 100 % structure rate over three attempts is not a reliability
   guarantee. `nemotron-3.5-lightning` would have shown 100 % on the right single
   attempt.
3. **`cost: n/a` does not mean free.** It means LiteLLM has no price table for
   that model. `ling-3.0-flash` and `granite-4.0-h-micro` are paid models whose
   real cost is unmeasured here; only the `:free` rows are genuinely zero.
4. **One response was truncated** at `max_tokens=16000` (`TASK_MAX_TOKENS`).
   Verbose reasoning models may need more headroom than the current ceiling for
   the larger programs.
5. **Latency is high across the board** — 24–74 s for the good models. These
   suit bulk and batch work, not an interactive loop.

## Open decision

Which model takes which role is the author's call (Rule 0). The evidence supports
a staged move: `worker` to a free or near-free model first, where a wrong answer
is cheap and visible, with `task` and `reflection` unchanged until a `BatchCompile`
trial run exists.

Rerun with `scripts/lm_bench.py`; re-fetch candidate ids first, since they drift.
