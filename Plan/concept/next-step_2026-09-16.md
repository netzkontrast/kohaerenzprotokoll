# What the old system already learned, and what the next step is

*Written 2026-09-16, after reading the retired `wiki-maintenance` skill, its
structure contract, the retired page schema, and the two live pilot runs.*

The retired system was thrown out for good reasons — it tried to do everything
at once and described more than it had. But **it ran twice, live, against real
documents, and measured what happened.** That evidence is not retired, and none
of it was carried across the reset. This document carries it, and then says what
the next step is.

Nothing below asks to rebuild the old machinery. Four of its findings change
what we do next; the rest is recorded so it is not re-derived at cost.

---

## 1. The number the whole plan turns on

Two live runs, seven documents, one process per call:

| | pilot (3 docs) | probe (4 docs) |
|---|---|---|
| merge model | sonnet | **haiku** |
| LM calls | 53 | — |
| wall time | 54 min | — |
| cost | **$8.38** | — |
| claims | 141 | 150 |
| concepts | 46 | 41 |
| score | 0.986 | 0.967 |

**$2.79 and 18 minutes per document.** Across the 409 landed that is roughly
**$1,140 and 125 hours**, and 46 of 53 calls were the merge step.

This is the single most important thing the old system knows. It means an
LLM pass over the whole corpus is a decision with a price tag, not a default —
and it is the author's decision, not a technical one.

It also settles a question we have been circling: **reading by hand is not a
temporary inconvenience before automation.** Three documents by hand cost
nothing and produced the note format, the `kind` field, the `[User Query]`
finding and two real conflicts. The same three documents through the pipeline
cost $8.38 and produced 141 claims of which 19 had quoting defects.

## 2. The old system split agreement from disagreement. We do the opposite.

Its concept-page contract said, in the schema itself:

> „the body asserts only what the sources agree on: no contradictory statement
> is ever rendered onto a concept page"

with every disagreement in a separate append-only ledger at
`Wiki/contradictions/<subject_kind>/<slug>.md`.

Our `Wiki/candidates/aegis.md` puts the conflict on the page, unmerged. **That
stays** — it is the author's decision and the reason the wiki exists.

But the ledger existed for a reason our page does not yet address, and the probe
run proves the reason is real:

> Two pairs are the same argument reached from different concepts —
> `die-13-alter`/`tsdp` on the alter count, `juna`/`moonshine-link` on the
> bond's origin. **That duplication is why the ledger is keyed by entity as well
> as by concept.**

**We already have this problem, on page one.** The emergence conflict on the
AEGIS page — emergence from `∅` versus emergence within the simulation — is
equally a conflict on an `Emergenz` page. Written twice, it will be reconciled
never, and a later source that settles it will be checked against one copy.

So: **conflicts get an id and one home, and term pages point at it.** See
decision 003. The page still shows the conflict in full — pointing is not
hiding — but the record of *that* conflict exists once.

The ledger's other property is worth keeping verbatim: **append-only.** A
resolved conflict moves from open to resolved with the resolution that settled
it, and is never deleted. It is „the memory that lets a later document be
checked against a clash found long before it arrived."

## 3. The citation checker is mostly a checker bug, and we know which one

`Citation resolution` sits in the `PRINCIPLES.md` catalogue as a thing to build.
The probe run built it, ran it, and measured what it got wrong:

| of 46 reported citation errors | |
|---|---:|
| matched once typographic quotes were normalised to ASCII | 19 |
| needed that **and** enclosing punctuation stripped | 21 |
| **genuinely wrong** | **6** |

**Seven out of eight reported errors were the checker's fault.** The source
export writes `„…"` with typographic glyphs, a page writes `"…"`, and a naive
comparison calls a verbatim quote a fabrication. The second class is subtler: a
page writing `the "Moonshine-Link," positioned as` against a source reading
`the "Moonshine-Link" is positioned as` — the comma belongs to the sentence
around the quotation.

A checker that cries wolf seven times out of eight gets switched off, and then
the six real ones ship. **So the normalisation is not a refinement to add later;
it is part of the first version.** And it must not go soft: the wording still
has to appear character for character after normalisation, an empty or
punctuation-only fragment is rejected outright, and `Nichts-Rauschens` still
fails against `Nichts-Rauschen`.

The residue is the real measurement: **6 fabricated or misplaced quotes in 150
claims, ~4%.** One quote was real but on line 54 while line 46 was cited; one
appeared in no cited file at all.

## 4. A source that claims authority must not be granted it silently

The most valuable defect the old system found, because it failed quietly:

One document — a „Hard-Canon" consolidation — asserts that any document
disagreeing with it is „to be marked [DEPRECATED] and archived". The pipeline's
instruction said resolution was pending „unless one source explicitly supersedes
the other". So the model read the claim, believed it, and marked the
disagreement resolved. The metric then computed *contested* from **pending**
disagreements — so that concept would have dropped out of the contested count,
out of the health view, and out of the author's review queue. **A second
disagreement kept the page visible by luck.**

The fix, and it is a rule rather than a code change: **a gather step proposes,
it never resolves.** Resolution is always pending. Contested is computed from
the *presence* of a disagreement, not its resolution.

That document is in our corpus. It is landed. Nothing currently stops the same
thing happening, because nothing currently reads it — but the first automated
gather will.

## 5. Kept, not acted on

Recorded so they are not re-derived. None changes the next step.

| what | where it came from | why keep it |
|---|---|---|
| **`protected: [reviewed]`** — a new source never overwrites a reviewed page, it *flags* it | retired lifecycle | Answers `review-promote.md`'s open question „does a promoted page ever go back" with a better answer than either option we listed |
| **Two status axes** — review state (`draft/reviewed/contested/…`) and evidence state (`high-confidence/single-source/tentative/contradicted`) are different fields | retired schema | We conflated them once already; the distinction is free if made early |
| **Page budgets, and that they were busted 4 of 4** | probe | Source pages ran 1,530–2,918 words against a 1,000 limit. Our `aegis.md` is ~1,100 words for *one* term. The budget question is real and unsettled |
| **The gate tension** | probe, left open | Candidate findings turned the whole repository's health check red, so no draft could be in flight without the gate being red. Settle it once: **lint gates promotion, not the repository** |
| **Quoting in the page's own prose is the dominant model defect** | pilot | 16–19 of 141 claims put a term in quotation marks that is not in the lines cited. P12 already forbids it; this is the measured rate it happens anyway |
| **Language discipline slips both ways** | probe | Claims written in German from an English source, and definition sentences not in English. Both directions need scoring, not one |

## The next step

Four moves, in order, each cheap and each producing evidence for the one after.

### A. Nineteen more pages by hand — with the conflict ledger from page one

`AEGIS` is written. `Entropie` (three senses) and `Emergenz` (the quiet
conflict) are next, and `Emergenz` is the one that forces the ledger to exist,
because its conflict is already on two pages.

Twenty pages is the point at which the schema gets written, per P3. **Not
before** — the old system's schema is 230 lines of YAML derived from a system
that promoted 2 pages out of 56.

Costs nothing. Produces the page format, the conflict record format, and the
term-selection judgement that no tool will make.

### B. Then the citation checker — and nothing else

One command, standard library, no LLM:

```
scripts/wiki.py check
```

- every `^[Sources/drive/<slug>.md:L-L]` resolves: file exists, lines exist
- the quoted fragment appears in those lines, **after** normalising typographic
  quotes, apostrophes and dashes to ASCII and stripping enclosing punctuation
- an empty or punctuation-only fragment fails
- every reading names a source that is in the manifest
- `MISSING`: terms in notes with no page, pages with no reading, conflicts with
  no ledger entry

It runs against the twenty hand-written pages first. **If it reports errors on
pages written by hand with care, the checker is wrong** — that is the
calibration the probe run had to discover the expensive way, and we get it free.

### C. Then measure one model against the hand-written notes

Three notes written by hand are a gold set of three. Small, but it is a real one
and it did not exist before.

The measurement is the *read-source* step, not the whole pipeline: given a
document, produce its note. Scored on what we now know matters — citations that
resolve, quotes that are in the lines, `kind` correctly identified, and whether
`[User Query]` provenance survives.

Cheap models first, through OpenRouter where it is easy and no DSPy is needed.
The old benchmark's lesson stands: report unreachable models as **NEVER
REACHED**, never as 0%.

### D. Then the budget is the author's decision

With B and C done, the question becomes answerable in the only form that
matters: *this model, on this step, produces notes of this quality, for this
much.* $1,140 and 125 hours was the old answer at the old settings. It may be an
order of magnitude cheaper. It is not a decision to take before it can be
priced.

## What this does not propose

- No re-adoption of the retired schema, lint, renderer, graph or codex.
- No `Wiki/terms/` yet. Promotion earns a directory when something is promoted.
- No commands. `review-promote.md` asks whether promotion needs a command at
  all; a file move does not.
- Nothing about the novel. It rests.
