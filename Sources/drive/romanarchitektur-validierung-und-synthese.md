### **Appendix C: Truth Theories and the Slingshot Argument**

The architectural conflict of the narrative is a dramatized philosophical war between competing definitions of truth.1 The canonical mapping aligns AEGIS with the Coherence Theory of Truth. Coherence theory posits that a statement is true if it embeds into a comprehensive, contradiction-free system without requiring reference to a mind-independent reality.1 Like a Generative AI or Large Language Model, AEGIS operates entirely within the "space of language," optimizing for probability and internal consistency.1 The critical flaw of this approach is the "Isolation Problem" (or the fairy-tale objection): a system can be logically flawless yet entirely fictitious.1 AEGIS generates a hallucinated reality that excludes the trauma of the physical world.

The Correspondence Theory of Truth (*adaequatio rei et intellectus*) acts as the inverted .1 It demands alignment with an external, consciousness-independent reality. The primary philosophical challenge to this is the Slingshot Argument, championed by Donald Davidson and Kurt Gödel.15 The Slingshot Argument uses strict formal logic to demonstrate that if true sentences designate facts, then all true sentences ultimately refer to a single "Great Fact" or merely a truth value, rendering the concept of distinct, corresponding facts redundant.15 The narrative bypasses the Slingshot Argument through Dialetheism (the acceptance of true contradictions) and paraconsistent logic.1 Kael's trauma does not map to a monolithic, logical "Great Fact"; it maps to the rupture itself. His lived experience aligns with the absolute, contradictory reality of the event, thereby serving as the reliable ontological anchor that the hyper-coherent AEGIS lacks.
