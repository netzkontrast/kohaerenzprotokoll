# Notes — Entropie aegis

**Document kind: a research brief, not a research result.** It commissions work on
entropy and states what the project already believes so the researcher can build
on it. Its answers are elsewhere. So its terms are *premises*, not findings, and
that is the strongest thing to know about every claim below.

## Terms it defines

**AEGIS** — the acronym is expanded and the function stated.
> „AEGIS = Autonomous Entropic Gatekeeper for Integrity Systems" ^[L19]
> „Seine Kernfunktion ist die **Regulation des Übergangs von Ordnung zu Unordnung
> (Entropie)**, um die Kohärenz und strukturelle Integrität des Gesamtsystems […]
> zu wahren. Es fungiert als **Schwelle und Filter gegen destabilisierende
> Prozesse**" ^[L21]

And, importantly, a *justification* for its existence:
> „Seine Existenzberechtigung leitet sich aus seiner Funktion in diesem
> **ständigen Kampf gegen die systemimmanente Tendenz zur Unordnung** ab." ^[L21]

**Überwelt** — the digital world AEGIS governs, and the place where the
order/entropy struggle is meant to be visible.
> „Als Domäne des Entropic Gatekeepers könnte die Überwelt selbst als ein Ort
> visualisiert werden, an dem der **permanente Kampf zwischen Ordnung und
> Entropie** sichtbar ist." ^[L97]

**Risse** — defined here as a consequence, which is the useful part.
> „Die \"Risse\" […] können nun als **direkte Manifestationen einer Zunahme von
> Entropie** interpretiert werden, die AEGIS' Kontrollkapazität übersteigt." ^[L94]

**Guardians** — only by their role, not their nature.
> „Dies prägt auch das Handeln der Guardians als spezialisierte Agenten dieses
> Entropie-Managements." ^[L93]

## Terms it uses without defining

Kern-Welten · Zero-Trust · Cognitive Firewall · Integrity Guardian · SIS ·
Negentropie · DID / dissoziative Identitätsstruktur · Multiplizität

The four AEGIS sub-functions appear as a bare list ^[L65] and are treated as
already known — so their definition is in an earlier document than this one.

## Naming — this document predates the current names

It calls the protagonist **Michael** ^[L95] and the external connection
**Julia** ^[L71, L96]. The current canon uses Kael and Juna. Dated 2025-04-17,
which is early in the corpus.

This is not an error to correct. It is a fact about the source, and the term
pages need it: a reading that says "Michael" and a reading that says "Kael" may
be the same claim about the same figure, and only the date reveals it.

## What it does not answer

By construction — it is asking, not telling. The open questions it poses are
themselves worth carrying, because several are still open in the project:

- Is dissociation an attempt to manage local entropy? ^[L67, L95]
- How does healthy complexity differ from destructive entropy? ^[L95]
- Does AEGIS perceive the external connection as an entropy injection, or as an
  order it cannot parse? ^[L71]
- How would AEGIS rate an alter's „Entropie-Signatur"? ^[L69]

The last one is a mechanism the project may never have decided.

## For the term pages

- `AEGIS` — one reading, foundational, with a dated justification clause.
- `Überwelt`, `Risse`, `Guardians` — one reading each, all defined *relative to*
  entropy, which suggests entropy is the root term rather than one among many.
- `Entropie` itself is **not defined here**. The document asks for a definition.
  A term page built only from this source would record a term the project builds
  everything on and cannot yet define — which is exactly the `MISSING` signal
  worth having.
