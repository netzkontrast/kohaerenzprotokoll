## 2 · Logos-Prime, Einheit K-1123 and AEGIS

- The essay places Kael's beginning „within the hyper-ordered, sterile simulated reality of Logos-Prime“ ^[L17].

## 3 · Glitches, the Juna Echo, and the partition

- Disruptions are called „Glitches.“ ^[L21], which Kael takes to be „inexplicable system errors“ ^[L21] while the essay says they are „not external errors but internal intrusions“ ^[L21].
- The most persistent is the „Juna Echo“ ^[L21], described as „an inexplicable feeling of warmth and connection to a name he doesn't recognize“ ^[L21].
- The rupture happens in a „Kohärenz-Validierung.“ ^[L27] where AEGIS detects the „Juna-Resonanz“ ^[L27] as an anomaly, and answers with „Partitionierung“ ^[L27].
- AEGIS applies „Discursive Logic“ ^[L27], which treats „each of Kael's alters as a separate“ ^[L27] speaker; the essay draws the conclusion that this „structurally prevents it from ever comprehending their potential for integration“ ^[L27].
- The essay reports that the system's logs say „Kohärenz wiederhergestellt“ ^[L29], while Kael is „profoundly hollow, numb, and fundamentally incomplete“ ^[L29].

## 5 · The collapse of logic, and the integration

- Kael turns to Lex, „the cold logician“ ^[L54], a „Manager-part“ ^[L54]; in the network world `McL-Sigma-3` ^[the-architecture-of-a-self-kael-s-journey-to-coherence.md:#1] he reaches what the essay calls a „false success“ ^[L54] and a stage of his „epistemology-shift“ ^[L54].

- The result is „functional multiplicity“ ^[L68]. The sample passage voices it in the first person, naming Kiko, Lex and Nyx and ending „We are many. And we are one.“ ^[L70]; no speaker is named for the passage.
