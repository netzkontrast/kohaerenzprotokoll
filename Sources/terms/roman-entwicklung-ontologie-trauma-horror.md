## Stance, read per passage

**Observed:** the document is a research report in four sub-questions and a synthesis; each sub-question has the same four-part build, and the headings carry a dash run in front (`\-----`) in the export. The first sub-question opens by naming a hypothesis it then tests, and the other three open with the same hypothesis label (L43, L80, L108): „Die rigorose epistemische Prüfung falsifiziert diese Hypothese“ ^[L15]. The first `Kernbefund` states the result in the report's own voice: „Die adversariale Hypothese wird falsifiziert“ ^[L31].

- **Report, with a tag.** The block headed `Kernbefund` states a finding and ends each sentence with the tag `\[H\]` ^[roman-entwicklung-ontologie-trauma-horror.md:#11]. The document does not say what the tag means; no line explains it.
- **Report on the document's own theory use.** The block headed `Das Unerwartete` presents a side finding the document calls unexpected; the first of its four blocks begins at L35 and applies a reported cognition model to dissociation.
- **Proposal for the novel.** The block headed `Konkret nutzbar` speaks to the novel in the conditional; a `Das Unerwartete` block does so too: „sollte nicht als psychologischer Prozess beschrieben werden“ ^[L104] and „Für den Roman bedeutet dies“ ^[L100]. These are the document's suggestions, not statements of a world.
- **Table.** L61 heads a column „Relevanz für den Roman (AEGIS)“ ^[L61]; its three rows (L62 to L64) assign each technique a use, e.g. „Sprache als defekter Algorithmus“ ^[L62].
- **Question.** One rhetorical question stands in the body, in the voice of the report: „Wie wird dieser fundamentale Nihilismus literarisch produktiv“ ^[L116]; a second is asked in the synthesis: „wie ist dann die Existenz von Juna zu erklären“ ^[L142]. The other question marks are in reference titles (L170, L172, L173).
- **Synthesis.** The final section states identities as facts of the novel's world, with no hedge and no tag, e.g. „AEGIS ist die makrokosmische Manifestation genau dieses Prozesses“ ^[L138]. Whether this is a plan or a description of an existing text is not said; the sentences stand in the present tense.
- **Reported theories.** Much of the body reports what cited writers say (the reports carry glued footnote numbers). A name or phrase inside those reports belongs to the reported text; this census lists only the concept names.
