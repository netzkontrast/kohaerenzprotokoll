## Stance, read per passage

The document describes itself as a plan for rewriting a narrative: „Diese Plotline strukturiert die Erzählung neu, berücksichtigt die Kritikpunkte und bietet konkrete Ansatzpunkte für eine Überarbeitung.“ ^[L13] It is ten scenes in three parts, each part headed in capitals, for example „TEIL: DIE KRISE UND DIE SCHERBEN“ ^[L154] (the export has dropped the digit of „TEIL 3“, which the source line has glued to the word).

Every scene has the same frame, which is a template and not a term: a `Ziel` ^[optimierte-plotline-genesis-der-existenz.md:#10], an `Anmerkungen` ^[optimierte-plotline-genesis-der-existenz.md:#10] block and a `Beats` ^[optimierte-plotline-genesis-der-existenz.md:#10] list. Under every beat a note in parentheses says what the beat is for, labelled `Annotation` ^[optimierte-plotline-genesis-der-existenz.md:#49]. So each beat has two voices, the story beat and the note about it. The Anmerkungen carry labelled advice: „Schwäche adressieren:“ ^[L24], „Stärke nutzen:“ ^[L25] and „Empfehlung:“ ^[L26], and once „Kritikpunkt:“ ^[L94], which asks to remove the numerical references.

The beats themselves are written in the present tense about an unnamed „Fragment“ ^[L36] and later about „Komponente 734“ ^[L125]; they are plan text for scenes, with questions inside them (L56 has the questions „Neugier? Gefahr?“ ^[L56], two of the nine question marks), and they are not a report of events.

**Export artifacts.** The export keeps the digits glued to words and drops them from the output of `--find`: the part and scene headings read „TEIL: …“ where the document has „TEIL 1“ to „TEIL 3“, and the scene titles quote the earlier narrative as `Genesis & 1` in the preview where the line has `Genesis 0 & 1`. Between the beat lists the export inserted the line `end list` as an HTML comment, 30 times (`read.py --count "end list"`). The quotation marks around terms are straight ASCII quotes, so the document's own inner quotes, for example around `Nichts Rauschens` and `Klick`, cannot sit inside a quotation of the note.

**What the document does not write.** The headings name the scenes of an earlier text as „Genesis“ and „Krise“ with Roman or Arabic numerals; those are references to another text and are not listed. `Kael` stands three times (L220, L222, L239) and is first named in a scene title; the document gives it no definition beyond „die Geburt von Kael als Mosaik traumatisierter, verwirrter Bewusstseinsfragmente“ ^[L222]. `Protokoll` is written without a longer name; what it is is said only by its effects (L197, L217, L218). `Qualia` is a borrowed concept the document uses as a given, once in a gloss on feeling and once as the reason AEGIS misreads the resonance (L58, L194).
