## Stance, read per passage

The document is one continuous expository text in four parts (headed `Teil I` at L18, and Teil II to IV at L75, L136 and L195), each part opened by a short paragraph that says what the part does, followed by numbered subsections. There is no first-person voice of an author in the body; the quoted address „Und Du? Was fühlst Du?“ ^[L209] is a proposed voice of the narration, offered as a possible mechanism, and not a passage of the text itself.

Parts I and II speak in the register of definition. Part I opens by saying it „etabliert die fundamentale „Physik“ des Romanuniversums“ ^[L22], and the quantum passage labels itself a metaphor: „nicht um eine buchstäbliche physikalische Erklärung“ ^[L44]. That sentence reports what the documents it was given lack and is the document's own claim about them.

The two tables are labelled by the document. `Tabelle 1` ^[L62] is called a „Leitfaden“ ^[L60] for weaving the reader's presence in as foreshadowing, so it speaks as a plan; its rows pair an anomaly with a world manifestation, a reader correlate, an effect on Kael and an effect on the attractors. `Tabelle 2` ^[L125] is a seven-column matrix whose column heads include „Wissenschaftliche Metapher“ ^[L129]; its cells are definitions and correspondences, not recommendations.

Part III speaks as a model: „Eros, Logos und Abyssos werden als drei konkurrierende seltsame Attraktoren“ ^[L156] modelled in a phase space. Its three bullets on the reader (L172 to L174) use the conditional „könnte die Anziehungskraft des Logos-Attraktors“ ^[L172] as their pattern, so they are hypotheses.

Part IV is a recommendation. It speaks in the register of necessity („Die Erzählung muss bewusst mit“ ^[L207] opens the first subsection's text) and in the label `Empfehlung 1` ^[L227]. The last paragraph claims an outcome of the proposal: „wird sichergestellt“ ^[L237].

A last block, headed `Referenzen` ^[L239], is a numbered list of entries: web pages with access dates, and a few titles of documents that read as project documents. It is the document's own source list and is not the text's argument; terms that stand only there (in titles and addresses) are listed in the next section.
