## Stance, read per passage

The document speaks as a guide to a later act of writing, in the imperative and the modal („sollte“, „sollten“), not as a scene. Its self-description is „detaillierte, fachkundige Anleitung zur Ausformulierung“ ^[L15], and it says it builds on an existing scene structure and on further context information that it cites by bracketed references (`[Kontext §4]`, `[665 ff., 706 ff.]`); the sources of those references are not in this document.

- L19 to L27, the setting passage: describes the construct city as a list of properties, then prescribes how it is to be shown. „Die Darstellung von KW sollte konsequent aus Kaels Perspektive erfolgen“ ^[L27] (the digit of the name is dropped by the quotation check).
- L29 to L44, the passage on Kael: a plan of how interior disturbance is to be rendered. „Kael ist der zentrale Wahrnehmungsfilter des Kapitels“ ^[L31].
- L46 to L79, the table: the document calls it a „Beat-by-Beat Execution Plan“ ^[L46] and a checklist; each row is a beat with focus, internal state, perception of the outside, technique, research number and an `Experten-Einblick` column ^[L54]. Many question marks in the rows (38 in the whole document, some of them in reference titles) follow a figure name, for example `Echo ?`, as an uncertain attribution of an intrusion; they are not questions put to the reader.
- L81 to L93, the passage on Juna: a plan, with a question the document leaves deliberately open: „Die Ursache für Junas Distanz sollte bewusst ambivalent gehalten werden“ ^[L85].
- L95 to L131, synthesis and pacing: a plan for rhythm and the arc of the beats.
- L133 to L144, recommendations: a numbered list of six, ending in a summary sentence.
- L146 to L179, references: titles and web addresses of cited texts; they are not the document's own terms and are not listed.
