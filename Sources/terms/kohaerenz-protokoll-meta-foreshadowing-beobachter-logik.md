## Stance, read per passage

The document stays in one stance, a plan for a writer, and marks the changes inside it
itself. Passage by passage:

- **L11 to L13, the frame.** An H1 that calls the text a strategy, and one sentence that gives
  a purpose (`Um den finalen Twist vorzubereiten`) and an obligation (`müssen wir` ... `einbauen`).
  The twist itself stands in an aside between dashes, as a given; the text does not say whether
  it is decided.
- **Sections 1 to 3, L15 to L35.** Each passage opens with a bold label. `Das Foreshadowing:`
  says what the text is to show, `Die Meta-Erklärung:` (sections 1 and 2) what lies behind it;
  section 3 has `Limit-Integration:` where a meta-explanation would stand. The Foreshadowing and
  Meta-Erklärung bullets are in the present tense; `Limit-Integration:` gives an instruction
  (`Nutze`, L35), and the lines that introduce sections 1 and 3 carry a modal (`sollte`, L17) and an
  imperative (`Integriere`, L32). The frame's obligation governs all of it.
- **Section 4, L37 to L42.** `Der Dialog-Hinweis:` opens on a conditional (`Juna könnte Kael
  fragen`) and gives a sample of her words; `Die Umkehrung:` says in the present tense what she
  makes clear.
- **Section 5, L44 to L49.** `Annotation:` opens on an obligation for one chapter (`In Kapitel 39
  müssen wir beschreiben`); `Der Schock:` says what Kael does.
- **The H3, L51 to L54.** Two bullets, each giving an instruction in the imperative (`Nutze`, L53 and L54).
- **L58.** One italic line after the `<!-- end list -->` marker of L56, with no label.

Four passages are samples of wording and not claims: L21, L41 and L53 in italics inside
quotation marks, and L58 in italics without them. Two of the four name a speaker in the sentence
that introduces them (L41: Juna; L53: Kael's inner voice). L21 and L58 name none, so the grammar
is their only label and this census supplies no speaker for either: L21 is third-person
narration that mentions Kael, and L58 is an imperative line that opens `STATUS:`. The H3 says
that italics are for `AEGIS-Protokolle` that query the `Beobachtungsstatus` (L54); L58 does not
say it is one.

No passage carries a date, a status or a claim about the document's own authority. The only other
document the text mentions is one it tells the writer to use (L35), and it restates nothing from it.

**What the briefing did not anticipate.**

1. A bold-label template whose two labels are the document's own method: what the text shows and
   what lies behind it. The briefing calls a label that repeats under every note `the template, not
   a term`; here the repeating pair is also the H1's subject, and the eight single labels are
   stance markers. The list lists the pair and not the eight, and says so above.
2. A joined name whose first half is quoted: the literal carries the closing glyph before the
   parenthesis, so the form written plain counts zero (above).
3. `capture.surfaces()` matching case-insensitively: `Sonde` gets `sondern` as a surface (above).
4. A profile line that reads a document's own part and chapter numbers as glued footnote numbers
   (above).
5. `read.py --find` refuses a two-letter quotation: `Du` returns `NOT IN THIS DOCUMENT` with L53 at
   100 % in common, because fragments under four characters are dropped. A term that short has to be
   cited through the phrase around it.
