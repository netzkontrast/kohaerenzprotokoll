## What the extraction ran into

**1. The labels are a schema, not a stance.** Sixteen repeated labels is the
densest marking of any document processed, and none of it says how to *read* a
passage. They are field names, filled uniformly for five Guardians and four
worlds. `profile.py` counts them correctly and calling them stance markers would
be wrong — **the same probe finds two unrelated things.**

**2. Two `### A.` headings in one section.** ^[L98] „A. Guardian Kairos" and
^[L110] `A. Guardian Sophia` both sit under ^[L94] section IV. Heading-based
addressing collides here, and the duplicate is in the source.

**3. The document's own count does not match its own content.** „die vier
zentralen Hüter" ^[L15] and „die vier Guardian/Welt-Paare" ^[L135], against
**five** named Guardians. The document flags it itself, in a parenthetical:
„Kairos und Sophia werden als zwei distinkte, aber komplementäre Guardians
dargestellt, die gemeinsam über diese Domäne wachen" ^[L96].

So *four pairs, five Guardians* is correct and the count is not an error — but
any tally taken from the prose without reading that parenthesis is off by one.

**4. Two names for one space, in the same sentences.** `Nexus` (11) and
`Überraum` (5); the field is called „Repräsentation im **Nexus**" ^[L29] while every
filled instance begins „Im **Überraum**…" ^[L29]. One document, two surfaces, no
statement that they are the same.

**5. Singular and plural of the central term split the count.** `Kern-Welten` 9,
`Kern-Welt` 9. An exact-string index sees two terms and halves each.

**6. Zero backslash escapes and one question mark.** The export left this file
clean, and the document asserts rather than asks — the opposite extreme on both
measures from anything processed so far.
