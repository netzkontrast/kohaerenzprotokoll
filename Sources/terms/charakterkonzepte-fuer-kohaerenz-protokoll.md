character's prose block rather than as a literal repeated line, so the label
counter undercounts a document that is in fact a filled template applied 21
times over.

**The document's own summary table hedges more than its prose.** Kael's
Kern-Welt is asserted plainly (`Konstrukt-Stadt (KW1)`, line 502), but every

similarly for Eos, Index and Silas. The one-Alter-one-world mapping this
document proposes is explicitly provisional in its own table, not only in
surrounding prose.

## What extraction ran into

**1. `Wächter` never names the collective Guardians in this document — it names
four different, smaller things.** The document's only proper name for the five
collective agents of AEGIS is the English loanword `Guardians` (line 59
onward). The German root `Wächter`/`Wärter` instead surfaces four separate
times for four different referents:

> „Limina (Die Wächterin / Schwellenhüterin)" ^[L263] — an individual Alter's
> own epithet.

> „…oder als bedrohliche Wächterfiguren an den Grenzen seines Bereichs." ^[L181]
> — one possible manifestation of the Guardian Cerberus.

> „Kann in KW1 als bürokratischer Kontrolleur, in KW3 als aggressiver Wächter
> auftreten." ^[L551] — the minor character Einheit 734's local guise in KW3.

> „Kann sich als Krieger, Wächter oder starkes Tier sehen." ^[L436] — Praetor's
> own possible internal self-image.

A fifth compound, `Grenzwärter` (Terminus's epithet, line 575), is explicitly
glossed by the document itself as a smaller-scale stand-in for one Guardian:

> „Repräsentiert die Funktion von Cerberus auf einer spezifischeren Ebene."
> ^[L578]

— not a fifth kind of thing. None of these five occurrences is the term
`Guardians` translated; each is a distinct, smaller referent that happens to
share the root.

**2. A substring/homonym trap: `Echo` names two unrelated things** (an Alter and
a manifestation of Juna), described above. `capture.py`'s exact-string count
cannot separate them; only reading each cited line can.

**3. AEGIS's acronym is spelled out once** (`Autonomous Entropic Gatekeeper for
Integrity Systems`, line 118, quoted above) and not repeated. This document is
the first read from `charaktere`, so whether this expansion matches, extends or
conflicts with any reading already on the AEGIS page is a reconciliation
question, not answered here.

**4. `Kohärenz Protokoll` carries two readings inside one document** — the
novel's own title, and, once (line 124, quoted above), the in-world name AEGIS
gives its own core programming. Flagged; not adjudicated here.

**5. The manifest's own `index_section` for this slug names two characters the
landed document does not contain**: `Charaktere, Anteile & Sprach-DNA (Kael,
Juna, Argus, Lex)`. Neither `Argus` nor `Lex` occurs in the document — the two
raw substring hits are `komplex`/`komplexe` (verified in `05-verify.txt`). This
is a fact about the manifest's section label against this one landed file, not
a comparison to another document, and is recorded here because the briefing
asks whether a stated count (or, here, a stated cast) matches the content.

**6. This is the first `charaktere` document read.** No candidate list from a
sibling document exists to compare against, and per the extraction rule none was
consulted before this list was frozen.
