# Term census — Entropie aegis

**This is not the note.** `Sources/notes/entropie-aegis.md` records what this
document *says*, selectively, because a note is a reading. This file lists
**every candidate term in the document**, including the ones a reading would
skip — because a term that looks unimportant here is exactly the term that gets
missed in the next document, where the conflict is.

Extraction is exhaustive on purpose. Judgement about whether something *is* a
term is recorded per row rather than applied as a filter.

## How to read the columns

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| **status** | `defined` — the document says what it is · `role` — says only what it does · `known` — used as already understood · `asked` — appears only inside a question · `external` — belongs to a discipline outside the project |
| **risk** | why this row might be handled inconsistently in the next document. The point of the file. |

## B. Entropy itself — the root term, and it is not defined here

`Entropie` appears **53 times in the body**, more than any other string in the
document, and **the document never defines it.** It commissions the definition:

> „Definieren und erklären Sie das Konzept der Entropie in der
> Thermodynamik/Statistischen Mechanik" ^[L29]

A case-insensitive search returns two more — the frontmatter's own title and
slug, which are provenance rather than source text (see F).

| sense asked about | lines | status |
|---|---|---|
| thermodynamic — „Maß für Unordnung/Zustandsvielfalt", Zweiter Hauptsatz | 29 | external, asked |
| information-theoretic — Shannon, „Maß für Unsicherheit" | 31 | external, asked |
| metaphorical — „psychische Entropie", social systems | 33 | external, asked |
| **the project's own** — what AEGIS regulates | 21,89,93 | **used as known, never defined** |

**This is the finding the census makes that the note did not.** The document's
own working sense of `Entropie` — the thing AEGIS fights — is used throughout as
if understood, while three *other* senses are explicitly commissioned. A term
page that recorded „this source defines entropy as X" would be wrong four ways.

## F. What the extraction itself ran into

**1. The frontmatter is inside the file.** Lines 1–9 carry `title` and `slug`,
so „Entropie aegis" ^[L3] and `entropie-aegis` ^[L4] both answer a search for the
term. Measured: `Entropie` occurs **53 times in the body and 54 in the file**,
and a case-insensitive search adds the slug on top. The inflation is small here
and is not always small — a slug is a term by construction.

Line numbers must still count from line 1, which is decided
(`Sources/README.md`), so **extraction skips lines 1–9 while citation does
not.** Two different line bases over one file, and that is the design rather
than an oversight.

**2. Substrings are not terms.** `Guardians` ⊄ `Integrity Guardian`;
`Negentropie` contains `entropie` and is its opposite. Three of the collisions in
this one document would be silently wrong under a substring matcher.

**3. Escaped markup sits inside the terms.** The converter wrote `\*` and `\[`
throughout (decision 002 leaves it). `\"Julia\"` at L71 is a quoted name with a
backslash in front of each quote mark. Any matcher must normalise that or miss
every quoted coinage in section E.

**4. A question is not a claim, and this document is 60% questions.** Lines
27–71 are the commission. `Alters`, `Entropie-Signatur` and `Negentropie` exist
*only* there. An extractor that treats every sentence alike will record four
terms this document asks about as four terms it asserts.
