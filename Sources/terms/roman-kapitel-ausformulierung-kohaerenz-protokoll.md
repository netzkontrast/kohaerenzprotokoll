## Stance, read per passage

The document is an instruction for writing one chapter and speaks throughout in the register of a plan: it says what the chapter "should" and "must" do, and it describes itself in its opening paragraph as „detaillierte, fachkundige Anleitung zur Ausformulierung des ersten Kapitels“ ^[L15]. Passage by passage:

**Opening paragraph (L13 to L15), a plan with a stated aim.** It names its aim as a chapter of „angestrebten Lesezeit von ca“ minutes ^[L15] and says it builds on an existing scene structure, „Es baut auf der bestehenden Szenenstruktur“ ^[L15]. The bracketed pointers (`[41-153]`) refer to other texts and are the document's report of them, not their words.

**Two pillar sections (L17 to L44), prescription about the setting and the figure.** The setting section reads KW1 as a mirror and an antagonist: the Konstrukt-Stadt „ist mehr als nur eine Kulisse“ ^[L21]. The section on Kael states his condition as fact in the plan: „Kael ist der zentrale Wahrnehmungsfilter des Kapitels“ ^[L31]. Its bullets are prescriptions, and the `[Kontext §4]`, `[Kontext §5]`, `[Kontext §7]` and `[665 ff., 706 ff.]` pointers mark lines taken from other texts.

**The table (L46 to L77), a checklist of 24 beats written as a plan.** Its heading is „Detaillierter Beat-by-Beat Execution Plan“ ^[L46]. It has seven columns; the column labels mix German and English (`Fokus (Focus)`, `Narrative Technik (Narrative Technique)`). Rows are numbered 1.1a to 1.15c. The cells hedge with question marks: an intrusion is attributed to a figure only as a question, as in „Echo von Silas? Host?“ ^[L56] and „Neugier (Host? Index?)“ ^[L64]. Those attributions are open suggestions, not statements. The cells that give an instruction to the writer are a plan; the cells that describe what Kael perceives are the plan's intended content, not a report of events.

**Juna section (L79 to L90), a plan that keeps a question open on purpose.** It says „Die Ursache für Junas Distanz sollte bewusst ambivalent gehalten werden“ ^[L81], then lists causes as a question. The four techniques below it are prescriptions.

**Synthesis and pacing sections (L92 to L113), prescriptions with reasons.** Both argue for a technique (filtered perception; a rhythm) and give examples to apply.

**Symbols section (L115 to L123), interpretation.** It says what the symbols carry: „tragen tiefere thematische Bedeutungen“ ^[L117], and then reads them as symptoms, consequence and solution.

**Closing recommendations (L125 to L139), plan and caution.** It restates the aims and hedges the narrator, „vermutlich eine enge dritte Person, limitiert“ ^[L133]. The title and L15 name the first chapter of the first part; no other chapter is named.
