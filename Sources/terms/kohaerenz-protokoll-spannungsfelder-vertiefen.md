The possessive is the largest: `Kaels` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#40] against the name alone. `Kernwelt` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#3] is mostly the plural `Kernwelten` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#20], which is listed separately, plus the compounds `Kernwelt-Herausforderungen` and `Kernwelt-Design`. `Exile` is mostly `Exiles`, also listed. Some of the gaps are export damage in the references, not the novel's vocabulary: `EP` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#8] has at least one more occurrence inside the file name `Cybernetics-EPST.pdf` at the end of the reference list, and `Manager` also counts inside the title `Managerial Practice` of a reference, itself a compound word counted for `Managerial` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#1].

**Abbreviation pitfall.** The pairings are written with `vs.` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#45], and that full stop reads as a sentence end to `capture.py`. No candidate follows `vs. ` directly in the list; the pairings were listed by their parts, and I checked the raw file with `grep` for `Lex`, `Kai` and `Co₁` that the counts of these short names match the lines (`Co₁` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#1], the subscript is in the file as written).

**Short names and escapes.** The short names `Lex`, `Nyx`, `Kai`, `EP`, `ANP`, `Ly` and `McL` were checked with `grep -w` on the raw file. The single letter `V` is not a candidate: it stands 31 times alone and far more inside other words, and the document writes the connection as `Verbindung V` and `Juna/V`. The table header cells carry escaped asterisks (`\*\*`) from the export; none sits inside a listed term.

**A name the title has and the body lacks.** The Drive title says `Spannungsfelder`, but the body writes `Spannungspunkte` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#10] and the word `Spannungsfelder` ^[kohaerenz-protokoll-spannungsfelder-vertiefen.md:#0] stands nowhere in it.

**Document vs. other voices.** Almost every sentence is the document's own analysis.
