## Stance, read per passage

The document is a world bible in English prose with German names in italics or parentheses. It reads throughout as description in the present tense, section by section; it marks no passage as plan, lock or question (it has 0 question marks, see the profile). The passages, in order:

- **Opening, L13.** The document calls itself „the canonical and authoritative reference“ ^[L13] for the narrative universe. That is the document's own claim about its standing; it is recorded here and not applied.
- **1.0 to 1.3, the metaphysical laws (L17 to L54).** Each concept is stated as fact of the fictional world: the primordial state „is not a passive vacuum“ ^[L23], The Foundation „is not a substance, a physical place, or a conscious entity“ ^[L33]. - **2.0 to 2.5, AEGIS.** The text says AEGIS „is the narrative's central antagonist“ ^[L60], gives its axiom in bold quotation marks at L66, and describes three layers (L74 to L76) with labels the document gives them: `Logics of Formal Inconsistency` in the first, `Discursive Logic` in the second, `Perverse Learning Loop` in the third.
- **3.0 to 3.4, System Kael.** A clinical frame: „Kael's mind is a case of“ ^[L104] Tertiary Structural Dissociation. The alters are presented in a table at L115 to L125 with a two-column header, one row per alter. After the table a paragraph at L127 sets the dyads Index and Nox, Silas and Oblivion against each other. Section 3.3 states a therapeutic goal, which it says „is not fusion“ ^[L131].

The section on the Risse says they have „a crucial dual nature“ ^[L169], and gives two perspectives, AEGIS's and Kael's, as two bullets.
- **5.0 to 5.1, the comparison.** A two-column table at L184 to L193 opposes two columns of worldviews. This is the document's own summary of the conflict, which it calls a „Grand Argument“ ^[L180]. The last line (L195) says the narrative's resolution decides which model is aligned with existence; it does not give the outcome.
