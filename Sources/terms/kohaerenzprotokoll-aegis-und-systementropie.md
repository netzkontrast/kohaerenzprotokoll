## What the extraction ran into

**1. German inflection defeats exact matching, and here it is decisive.**
`Thermodynamik` as a noun occurs **zero times**. The concept is present five
times, as `thermodynamischen` (4) and `thermodynamischer` (1). A census matching
strings would record the term as absent from a document that argues about it.

Worse for a term that matters: **`emergent` appears in seven distinct surface
forms** — Emergente, Emergentes, emergente, emergentem, emergenten, emergenter,
emergentes — 13 occurrences, beside 13 of the noun `Emergenz`. A string-keyed
index sees eight terms where there is one.

**2. The document abbreviates its own central term halfway through.**
`Kael-Julia-Bindung` 16 times, then `K-J-Bindung` 5 times from ^[L151] onward.
Nothing announces the switch.

**3. The stance marker is invisible to the probe.** `repeated labels` reports
`none` against 26 `[User Query]` markers, because they are inline rather than
bold headings. **`none` means "no convention this probe knows about", not
"unmarked"**, and this document is the proof.

**4. 110 reference numbers are glued to the words they annotate**, and **96
backslash escapes** sit inside the text — the highest of any document processed
so far. `\"Entropie\"` and `\[User Query\]` both carry them, so the stance marker
itself needs normalising before it can be found.

**5. 26 typographic marks against 122 ASCII quotes.** The document mixes both
conventions, so a quote-matching check meets both glyph families in one file.
